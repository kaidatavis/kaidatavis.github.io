Students

Project ideas for PhD, internship and MSc students, with scholarship and application details.

Internship

I welcome internship students. The internships are usually online/remote and without pay. This can be during the summer holiday or during the semester while you are studying. Please contact me if you are interested in one of the project ideas listed below. Currently I do not have capacity for other project ideas, unless it is about agentic science 😁 (please see the details below).

PhD

I usually would have an online meeting first so we can have a quick chat. After that I may request you to work with me on a research project for a few months if time allows (similar to the Internship mentioned before). This would allow us to know each other a bit better, and especially important if we never meet before or you need scholarship.

PhD - Self-Funded

You will have a much better chance of being accepted if you can fund your study. There are Teaching Assistant opportunities, but these will not be enough for the tuition and living expenses.

If this fits you, please see below a list of projects I am actively working on and looking for students. You can also propose your own project ideas and I will be happy to discuss them.

PhD - Scholarships Required

UK students

There are often PhD scholarship opportunities only for UK students and they are not as competitive. Please contact me by email for details.

Non-UK Students

CSC Scholarship for Chinese students

As a partner university with China Studentship Council (CSC), University is allocated up to 10 studentships each year. The studentship jointly sponsors the full-time 36-month PhD programme, with CSC covers the stipend (e.g. living fees, international airfare, medical insurance), and University of Nottingham waives all the tuition fees. Information of eligibility and application process can be found at the University of Nottingham CSC web site.

Other Scholarships

Scholarships for non-UK students have become very competitive recently. You will need two or three publications in top conferences or journals to have a realistic chance. This is a very high bar, but recent students who received scholarships met or exceeded this.

Project Ideas

Human-Centred Agentic Science explores how AI agents can support researchers across the stages of scientific discovery, from literature research and hypothesis generation to implementation, evaluation and writing. The aim is to let AI take on time-consuming work while researchers guide the process, apply their expertise and make the important decisions — not to automate science end-to-end. Read more about Human-Centred Agentic Science ↗

Below are more specific project ideas, all under the Human-Centred Agentic Science:

Idea 1: Human-Centred Agentic Science for Literature Research

Develop tools that help researchers explore a field, understand relationships across papers and identify research gaps. Agents can iteratively suggest topics and relevant work, while researchers steer the exploration with feedback and draw their own conclusions. This could build on focused literature collections and systems such as vitaLITy 2 and AwesomeLit. Read more about this project idea ↗

Idea 2: Human-Centred Agentic Science for Quantitative Research

There are many different types of quantitative researches. Here we use human-guided machine learning for automated trading as an example: combining market data with news sentiment, exploring reinforcement-learning and time-series anomaly-detection approaches, and building interactive tools to compare and tune models. The focus is on helping researchers inspect performance and guide experimentation, rather than assuming a model will reliably make profitable trades. Read more about this project idea ↗

Idea 3: Human-Centred Agentic Science for Qualitative Research

Explore how LLMs can assist with repetitive parts of qualitative analysis, such as first-pass coding and checking consistency across themes or coders. Researchers review the evidence and recommendations, retaining control over interpretation and decisions. One example is Co-Refine, which flags possible coding inconsistencies during analysis. Read more about this project idea ↗
